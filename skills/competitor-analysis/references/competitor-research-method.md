# Competitor Research Method

Use this method to create a decision-ready comparison without overstating what the sources prove.

## Start with the Decision

A useful comparison helps someone choose. State:

- The decision.
- The target customer.
- The problem or progress being considered.
- The geography and time boundary.
- The options already under consideration.
- The evidence that could change the decision.

Do not research every visible competitor. Choose the smallest set that represents the meaningful ways customers solve the problem.

## Types of Competition

### Direct

Serves a similar customer with a similar solution.

### Indirect

Creates a similar outcome through a different product, service, or process.

### Manual

Spreadsheets, documents, messages, or repeated human work.

### Internal

Hiring, assigning the task to an employee, or building a custom solution.

### Delay or No Action

Accepting the current cost, postponing the decision, or lowering the goal.

Customer interviews often reveal the true alternative. Include it even when it has no company website.

## Source Priority

Use the best source for each claim:

1. Official pricing, product, documentation, policy, or release pages.
2. Official company announcements for launch or change claims.
3. Direct customer evidence supplied by the user.
4. Credible first-party customer stories, with the company's selection bias noted.
5. Reputable independent reporting or analysis.
6. Review platforms and community posts for experience signals, treated cautiously.
7. Search snippets only as leads to the original page.

Several articles repeating the same announcement are one evidence chain, not independent confirmation.

## Claim Ledger

Track material claims using:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed | Directly supported by a suitable source |
| Contested | Credible sources disagree or experience evidence challenges the claim |
| Inferred | Reasonable interpretation that the source does not state directly |
| Unverified | Important claim with insufficient current evidence |

Record the source URL and access date. Add the page or event date when it affects freshness.

Assign a stable claim ID and cite it beside each material comparison claim. For supplied private evidence, use a file title and section or page reference rather than inventing a public URL. Record the supplied document date and review date; do not label the review date as a live website access date.

Distinguish evidence that a company makes a claim from evidence that the promised outcome occurs. A vendor case study is selected evidence with limited generalizability. If sources disagree, check plan, region, date, and use case before calling the claim contested.

## Pricing Rules

Record:

- Amount and currency.
- Per-user, per-month, per-project, usage-based, or other basis.
- Monthly or annual billing.
- Starting price, range, or exact tier.
- Taxes, minimums, add-ons, or usage limits when shown.
- Whether a sales call is required.
- Access date.

Use `Not publicly disclosed` when the source withholds price. Do not calculate an implied price without enough information.

An inaccessible pricing page means `Unable to verify`, not `Not publicly disclosed`. Keep currencies and plan scopes separate unless a justified conversion or normalization is stated. Label any derived amount and show the calculation, including annual commitments or minimum seats.

## Comparison Criteria

Choose criteria from the customer evidence and decision. Useful examples:

- Customer and use case.
- Trigger and desired outcome.
- Method or delivery model.
- Setup and adoption effort.
- Important capabilities.
- Meaningful constraints.
- Pricing and buying motion.
- Proof and trust signals.
- Switching cost.
- Objections addressed.

Define each criterion before scoring or comparing. Avoid numeric scores unless the user provides a meaningful scale and evidence for each rating.

## Evidence Traps

### Website Copy as Proof

A homepage can confirm how a company describes itself. It cannot independently confirm that customers achieve the promised result.

### Absence as Weakness

A missing homepage mention may appear in documentation, a higher tier, a sales-led plan, or a recent release. Report where you looked.

### Feature Count as Strategy

More listed features do not establish better customer fit. Compare the features that matter to the customer's progress and constraints.

### Stale Pricing

Undated roundups and search snippets frequently lag current pricing. Verify against an official page.

### Empty Category Language

Repeated phrases can reveal category conventions, but repetition does not prove customer importance. Compare them with interview evidence.

## From Gap to Hypothesis

Use three levels:

1. **Finding:** What the reviewed sources show.
2. **Implication:** Why the finding matters for the named decision.
3. **Hypothesis:** A possible opportunity that needs testing.

Illustrative example only, not actual research or customer evidence:

- Finding: Four reviewed platforms emphasize dashboard creation, while three of five supplied interviews describe the burden of explaining results to clients.
- Implication: Communication support may matter alongside reporting automation.
- Hypothesis: A guided narrative layer may produce stronger pilot interest than another dashboard feature.

The hypothesis still needs customer or market validation.

## Stop Rule

Stop researching when:

- Every material comparison row has a credible source or an explicit unknown.
- New sources repeat established facts.
- The key disagreement is clear.
- The next decision can be made or the next evidence-producing action is obvious.

Continue when a material current claim rests only on a snippet, stale page, secondary repetition, or unsupported inference.
