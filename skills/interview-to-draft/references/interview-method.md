# Adaptive Interview Method

Use the interview to retrieve substance that generic generation cannot supply. Ask one question at a time and choose the next question from the answer just received.

## Question sequence

Begin with the highest-value missing element. A useful default progression is:

1. **Position:** What does the speaker actually believe or recommend?
2. **Example:** When did this become visible in real work?
3. **Mechanism:** What happened, in what order, and why?
4. **Judgment:** What did the speaker choose, reject, or change?
5. **Boundary:** Where does the idea fail or stop being useful?
6. **Reader consequence:** What should the intended reader do differently?

This is a routing guide, not a fixed questionnaire. Skip anything already supported.

## Follow-up types

Use the smallest follow-up that resolves the gap:

- **Definition:** "When you say reliable, what must be true?"
- **Concrete example:** "What is one real instance where this happened?"
- **Sequence:** "What happened immediately before and after that?"
- **Evidence:** "What did you see that convinced you?"
- **Decision:** "What did you change because of it?"
- **Contrast:** "What did you try first, and why did it fail?"
- **Boundary:** "When would you avoid this approach?"
- **Reader application:** "What is the smallest version someone could try?"

Ask for artifacts when they would be stronger than recollection: a draft, screenshot, report, prompt, dataset, email, or before-and-after example.

## Avoid leading the speaker

Do not place a finished argument inside the question. Prefer:

> What do you think caused the draft to fail?

Avoid:

> Was the draft bad because the AI lacked enough context and personal examples?

Offer a tentative interpretation only to resolve ambiguity:

> I heard two causes: weak research and a missing personal example. Which one mattered more?

## Preserve useful language

Record the answer before summarizing it. Preserve distinctive phrases, uncertainty, disagreement, and spoken energy. Clean filler in the draft, not in the evidence record.

Never convert a paraphrase into quotation marks. Mark uncertain names, figures, dates, or claims for verification.

## Readiness test after each answer

Track:

- What is now supported?
- What remains generic?
- Which claim still lacks an example or source?
- What would change the reader's understanding most?

Ask the next question that closes the most important gap.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the readiness gate in `SKILL.md` passes. Also stop when:

- The user asks to stop.
- The user cannot answer and the draft can be narrowed honestly.
- The remaining gap requires research or an unavailable artifact.
- Twelve substantive questions have been asked. At that point, explain the gap and offer to continue or narrow.
