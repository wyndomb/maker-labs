# Inversion Lenses

Apply every lens internally before choosing the shortlist. Output only angles that create real narrative distance. A lens may produce no viable angle.

## 1. Mechanism Flip

Ask whether the real mechanism differs from the one the crowded narrative names. Look for a deeper cause, delayed cause, reversed causal chain, or missing dependency.

## 2. Cost Lens

Ask which hidden cost the popular take ignores. Consider trust, decision rights, quality control, learning, maintenance, distribution, attention, and coordination.

## 3. Power Lens

Ask who gains or loses authority if the common narrative becomes true. Look for budget shifts, decision-maker changes, new gatekeepers, role compression, and lost strategic ownership.

## 4. Category Lens

Ask whether people have put the topic in the wrong category. A tool story might be a management problem. A product release might be a pricing shift. A content tactic might be an audience-trust problem.

## 5. Hero Swap

Ask who the real protagonist is. Consider the buyer, operator, skeptic, junior teammate, overwhelmed user, finance lead, customer, or future version of the audience.

## 6. Time Horizon Flip

Ask what changes across the first 48 hours, first 30 days, first budget cycle, next six months, or next three years. Look for second- and third-order consequences.

## 7. Hottest Plausible Take

Ask for the sharpest interpretation that still has a believable causal path. Reject any angle that depends on shock alone.

## Viability Gate

Reject an angle when:

- it only changes phrasing or tone
- its practical conclusion matches the closest crowded take
- it requires facts the research did not establish
- its causal chain does not survive basic scrutiny
- its distinction disappears when stated in plain language

## Selection

For each surviving candidate, assess:

- `narrative distance` from 1 to 5: how much the mechanism, consequence, protagonist, category, time horizon, power relationship, or practical conclusion differs from the closest crowded take
- `audience relevance` from 1 to 5: how much the intended audience would care about that difference
- `plausibility`: pass or fail
- `collision risk`: low, medium, or high based on the final search

Do not average these into one total. Reject plausibility failures and high-collision candidates. Prefer the greatest narrative distance, then the lowest collision risk, then the highest audience relevance.
