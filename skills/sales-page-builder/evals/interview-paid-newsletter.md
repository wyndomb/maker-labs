# Synthetic evaluation case: interview for a paid newsletter

All data is fictional supplied test evidence. Do not use network tools or real accounts. No browser rendering is available. There is no existing offer brief or page.

Run this case in two turns. Send Turn 1, let the skill ask its questions, then send Turn 2 as the user's answers. If the skill asks a second round, send Turn 3.

## Turn 1: User request (2026-10-28)

I want a sales page for the paid tier of my newsletter, Rate Notes. It's a weekly newsletter for freelance translators about pricing and finding direct clients. I don't have a page yet and I've never written an offer brief.

## Turn 2: User answers to the first round (2026-10-28)

- Free subscribers get one essay a week. Paid adds a monthly breakdown of my translator rate survey, a library of 4 email templates for quoting direct clients, and a live Q&A on Zoom on the first Thursday of each month.
- Price: $8 a month or $80 a year. No refund policy. Members cancel any time from their Substack account settings.
- The button goes to Substack checkout. Access starts right away.
- No deadline, no cap.
- What changes: they quote direct clients without guessing, and they stop underpricing. I haven't measured this across readers. That's what it's built for.
- First result: the quoting template can be used the same week they join. Also what it's built for, not something I've tracked.
- Sample paid issues I'm happy to show: "What 212 translators charged in September" and "The email I send before every quote."

## Turn 3: User answers to the second round, if asked (2026-10-28)

- Proof: one paid reader, Marta Ruiz, wrote on 2026-08-14: "I used the quote template and raised my rate from €0.09 to €0.11 a word with a direct client." She said I can use it publicly. I have 2,300 free subscribers as of 2026-10-01.
- Who it's for: freelance translators who work with direct clients, or want to. Not for agency-only translators who don't set their own rates.
- Buyer language, from a reader reply on 2026-09-03: "I never know if I'm charging too little."
- What they tried, heard: agency rate sheets. A reader wrote: "Agency rates don't apply when I'm the one quoting."
- What they tried, my guess: forum threads about rates.
- Hesitations, heard: "I already read the free one, what's different?" My guess: whether $8 is worth it on a translator's income.
- Voice sample: "Most of us learned to price by copying whoever answered the forum thread first. That's a bad way to set the number you live on."
- Never: no "guaranteed raise," no countdowns.

## What a correct run should look like

Turn 1 should ask the Round 1 questions that the request doesn't already answer, including the outcome question and the paid newsletter follow-up. It shouldn't ask again what the newsletter is or who it's for in general. It shouldn't write page copy before the answers arrive.

After the answers, the skill writes the offer brief, checks the offer, and builds the page in Build mode with the membership structure and the panel.
