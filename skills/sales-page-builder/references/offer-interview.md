# Offer Interview

Use this to fill the offer brief. Ask in at most two rounds. Skip anything the user already answered or a supplied file covers. Short answers are fine. A missing answer becomes `[CONFIRM]` in the brief.

## Round 1: The offer (always ask what's missing)

1. **What are you selling, in one sentence?** Name, format, and who it's for.
2. **What does the buyer get?** Deliverables, sessions, files, access, support, duration. If you have the product itself, a curriculum, or a module list, share that instead of describing it.
3. **What does it cost, and how do they pay?** Price, payment plans, or how the price gets set (for example "quoted after a call"). Refund policy, if one exists.
4. **What happens after someone clicks the main button?** Checkout, booking calendar, application form, email reply. And how fast?
5. **Is there a real deadline or limit?** Enrollment close date, cohort size, event date, price increase date. "None" is a fine answer.

## Round 2: Proof, buyers, voice (ask what's missing)

6. **What proof do you have that it works?** Customer or student quotes (with names, and whether you can show them publicly), results with numbers and dates, your own use of the thing, audience size, press. Note where each one came from.
7. **Who is it for, and who is it not for?** The buyer's situation before buying, in their own words if you have them (reply emails, call notes, survey answers).
8. **What have buyers already tried, and why didn't it work?** The usual fixes, tools, or courses they reach for before finding you. Mark which ones you've heard from buyers and which you're guessing. One real answer beats three guesses.
9. **What do people hesitate about before buying?** Mark which ones you've actually heard and which you're guessing.
10. **Paste a sample of your writing you're happy with.** A newsletter, a post, an email. Two or three paragraphs is enough. Optional: a link to someone else's sales page you admire, for structure and pacing.
11. **Anything this page must never do?** Tactics you refuse, words you hate, claims you can't make for legal or personal reasons.

## Reading Answers Into The Brief

- Record each answer with its source: "user, [date]" for chat answers, or the file name or URL.
- Split proof by whether it's about **this offer** (a customer result) or about **the seller** (audience size, your own system). Pages often lean on seller proof when buyers need offer proof. The audit flags the gap.
- Objections the user has heard go under **Heard**. Guesses go under **Hypothesis**. Both are useful. Only heard ones count as buyer language.
- Answers to "what have they tried" go under **What They've Tried** with the same Heard or Hypothesis label. Record why each attempt fell short, in the buyer's words when the user has them.
- Product files (the template, the curriculum, a module list) are the source for deliverables. Cite the file name.
- A page the user admires goes under **Reference Page**, with what to borrow from it: section order, section length, pacing. Nothing on that page is a fact about this offer. Its claims, quotes, prices, guarantees, bonuses, and deadlines never enter the brief.
- If the user's answers contradict the live page (a different price, a different number), keep both, note the conflict, and ask which is current.
- Don't fill gaps from the page itself. A number on the page is a claim to verify. It isn't a source for itself.
