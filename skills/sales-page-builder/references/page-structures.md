# Page Structures

Starting templates by offer type. Each section lists what it must do and which part of the offer brief feeds it. Drop or merge any section the brief can't fill, or mark it "collect first." These are common practitioner patterns, not proven formulas.

## Adjusting Any Template

- **How much the visitor already knows.** Cold traffic (search, ads, a stranger's share) needs the problem named and the seller introduced early. A warm list that already trusts the seller can start with the offer and get to the price sooner.
- **Price and commitment.** The higher the price or the bigger the commitment (time, a sales call, a cohort schedule), the more proof, process detail, and objection handling the page needs before the ask. A free signup needs almost none.
- **Repeat the CTA** after each section where a ready buyer might decide: after the offer summary, after proof, and at the end. Keep the wording and destination identical each time.
- **What buyers already tried.** Sections that name a failed attempt draw from the brief's What They've Tried. State a heard entry plainly. Write a hypothesis only as a condition ("If you've tried a planner app and dropped it by week two..."), or leave it out. Use as many entries as the brief has, even if that's one. Pair each with what this offer does differently, from the brief.
- **A page the user admires.** When the brief has a reference page, move the template toward its section order, section length, and pacing where they fit this offer type. Borrow structure only. None of its lines, claims, quotes, prices, guarantees, bonuses, or deadlines carry over. If its structure calls for a section the brief can't fill, such as a bonus stack or a countdown, drop it or mark it "collect first."
- **Homepages doing sales work** need the offer and its CTA high on the page. Writing, social links, and the about section come after the ask, or sit in clearly secondary positions.

## Service (Done-For-You, Consulting, Productized Service)

1. **Hero:** the outcome, who it's for, and the main CTA with an honest label. *Brief: outcome, audience, next step.*
2. **The problem in their words:** the situation buyers are in before hiring, and what they've already tried. *Brief: buyer language (heard), what they've tried, audience.*
3. **What the engagement is:** scope, duration, deliverables, what the client puts in. *Brief: deliverables, client time.*
4. **How it works:** three to five steps from first call to handover. *Brief: process.*
5. **Proof from clients:** results, quotes, before-and-after. If none exist, use the seller's own use of the same system, labeled as theirs, and mark client proof "collect first." *Brief: offer proof.*
6. **Who it's for and not for.** *Brief: who it's for, who it's not for.*
7. **Price or pricing method.** *Brief: price.*
8. **FAQ from real objections.** *Brief: objections, heard ones first.*
9. **The ask:** the form or calendar, plus what happens next and how fast. *Brief: next step, response time.*
10. **About the seller:** short. Credibility tied to this service. *Brief: seller proof.*

## Course Or Cohort

1. **Hero:** what students will be able to do afterward, the format, and the CTA. *Brief: outcome, format.*
2. **The stuck moment:** what the student is trying and why it isn't working. *Brief: buyer language, what they've tried.*
3. **What changes:** the before and after, concretely. *Brief: outcome.*
4. **What you'll learn or build:** a curriculum by week or module, with what each one produces. *Brief: deliverables.*
5. **Format and schedule:** dates, time zones, live or recorded, time per week, replay access. *Brief: format, dates.*
6. **Instructor:** why this person can teach it. *Brief: seller proof.*
7. **Student proof:** quotes and results from past students. For a first cohort, use pilot feedback or the instructor's own results, labeled honestly. *Brief: offer proof.*
8. **Who it's for and not for.** *Brief: who it's for, who it's not for.*
9. **Price, payment options, and refund policy.** Only the policies that actually exist. *Brief: price, refund.*
10. **FAQ.** *Brief: objections.*
11. **Enrollment:** CTA plus the real close date or seat limit, if there is one. *Brief: deadline.*

## Digital Product (Template, Toolkit, Guide, Swipe File)

1. **Hero:** what it is, what it lets them do, a preview image, and the CTA. *Brief: deliverables, outcome.*
2. **Is this you?** (optional): two or three specific situations a buyer would recognize, each built from buyer language or a heard entry in What They've Tried. Drop this section if the brief has neither. Never invent personas. *Brief: buyer language, what they've tried, audience.*
3. **What they've tried** (optional): the usual fixes that fall short, and what this product does differently. *Brief: what they've tried.*
4. **What's inside:** a list with one line on each item, taken from the product files when the user supplied them. *Brief: deliverables.*
5. **Preview:** screenshots or sample pages. *Brief: assets.*
6. **What you can do with it:** two or three concrete uses. *Brief: outcome, buyer language.*
7. **Who made it:** short. Why this person can make this product, using seller proof tied to this topic. For cold traffic, move it up beside the hero. *Brief: seller proof.*
8. **Proof.** *Brief: offer proof.*
9. **Price and refund policy.** *Brief: price.*
10. **FAQ:** format, compatibility, updates, license. *Brief: objections.*
11. **CTA.**

## Membership Or Paid Newsletter

1. **Hero:** what members get, how often, and the CTA. *Brief: deliverables, cadence.*
2. **Free vs paid:** exactly what the upgrade adds. *Brief: tier differences.*
3. **Samples:** links to or excerpts from paid issues or resources. *Brief: assets.*
4. **Proof from members.** *Brief: offer proof.*
5. **Price, billing, and how to cancel.** *Brief: price, cancellation.*
6. **FAQ.** *Brief: objections.*
7. **CTA.**

## Workshop, Webinar, Or Live Event

1. **Hero:** what attendees will make or learn, the date, time and time zone, and the CTA. *Brief: outcome, date.*
2. **Agenda.** *Brief: agenda.*
3. **Host.** *Brief: seller proof.*
4. **Who it's for.** *Brief: who it's for, who it's not for.*
5. **Price, or free, plus the replay policy.** *Brief: price, replay.*
6. **Proof from past events.** *Brief: offer proof.*
7. **CTA, with the registration close if it's real.** *Brief: deadline.*

After the event date passes, the page needs to change, redirect, or become a replay page. The audit flags expired event pages.

## Lead Magnet Or Free Signup

1. **Headline naming the thing:** "Get the [specific thing] for [specific situation]." *Brief: deliverable.*
2. **What's inside:** three bullets. *Brief: deliverables.*
3. **Preview image.** *Brief: assets.*
4. **Form:** usually email only, plus anything genuinely needed for the next step. *Brief: next step.*
5. **What happens after:** when it arrives, and what else they'll get, such as a newsletter. *Brief: next step.*
6. **Privacy line.**
