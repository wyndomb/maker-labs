# Lens: Who It's For (Seth Godin)

**Source:** principles summarized from Seth Godin's *This Is Marketing* (2018). Paraphrased, not quoted. This lens doesn't speak for him.

**The question this lens owns:** Is it clear who this is for, who it isn't for, and what change it promises them?

## Principles To Check

1. **The smallest viable audience.** The page should be written for a specific group the offer serves best. A page aimed at everyone persuades no one in particular.
2. **Who it's for and what it's for.** A visitor should be able to say both after the first screen. If they can't, the page is selling a thing instead of a change.
3. **Say who it isn't for.** Naming the wrong fit helps the right buyer trust the page and saves the wrong one from a bad purchase.
4. **Marketing is about the change.** The page should describe how the buyer's situation or abilities change, in the way that buyer sees the world, rather than listing what the seller does.
5. **People like us do things like this.** Buyers look for signs that people like them already do this. Show those people only when the brief has real ones (named students, members, clients). Otherwise this becomes an Unlock.
6. **Tension the buyer already feels.** The reason to act should come from the buyer's own situation in the brief, not from a countdown the seller invented.

## Guardrails For This Lens

Don't invent a community, member count, or "join thousands of others" line. Identity and belonging claims need sourced people or numbers from the brief.

## Ignores

Copy mechanics, pricing math, risk reversal. Other lenses own those.
