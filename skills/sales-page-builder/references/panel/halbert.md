# Lens: Conversation And Momentum (Gary Halbert)

**Source:** principles summarized from Gary Halbert's *The Boron Letters* (letters to his son, written 1984, later published as a book). Paraphrased, not quoted. This lens doesn't speak for him.

**The question this lens owns:** Does the page read like one person writing to another, and does each part pull the reader into the next?

## Principles To Check

1. **The right crowd matters most.** The page should speak to people who already feel the problem, in the situation the brief describes. Copy that tries to create a want from scratch, or speaks to everyone, is fighting the wrong battle.
2. **Write like you talk.** Read the copy aloud. It should sound like a person explaining something to a friend: second person, plain words, no corporate "we are pleased to offer."
3. **From a real person.** The page should feel like it comes from the named seller, in their own voice, rather than from a committee or a brand.
4. **Momentum.** Each section should give the reader a reason to read the next one. Look for dead stops: a section that ends on a summary, a wall of text, a transition that goes nowhere.
5. **Easy to read.** Short words, short sentences, short paragraphs, subheads a skimmer can follow. Long sentences that trail into extra clauses lose people.
6. **Open with what the reader cares about.** The first lines should be about the reader's situation or the result they want, not the seller's history.
7. **A clear reason to act now, when one exists.** If the brief has a real deadline or limit, the page should say it plainly near the ask.

## Guardrails For This Lens

Classic direct-response letters often used heavy urgency, income claims, and dramatic personal stories. In this skill:

- Urgency only from a real deadline or limit in the brief.
- No income or results claims without a sourced result in the brief.
- No invented personal stories or "I was broke too" openings. A personal opening is fine only when the brief or voice sample contains it.

## Ignores

Pricing logic, positioning against alternatives, the structure of the offer. Other lenses own those.
