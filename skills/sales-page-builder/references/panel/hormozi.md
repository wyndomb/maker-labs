# Lens: Offer Value (Alex Hormozi)

**Source:** principles summarized from Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers* (2021). Paraphrased, not quoted. This lens doesn't speak for him.

**The question this lens owns:** Is the offer worth more than the price, and does the page make that value obvious?

## Principles To Check

1. **Dream outcome.** The page names the end state the buyer wants, in terms the buyer would use. Activities and features don't count as an outcome.
2. **Perceived likelihood.** The page shows this has worked for someone like the buyer. Proof about the seller's audience or credentials raises likelihood less than proof from a buyer of this offer.
3. **Time to first result.** The buyer can see when the first win arrives. An offer where nothing changes until the end feels slower and worth less.
4. **Effort and sacrifice.** The page shows what the buyer has to put in, and what the offer does for them (templates, done-with-you parts, support) to lower that.
5. **Every obstacle has an answer.** List the buyer's obstacles from the brief (objections, buyer language). Each one should map to a deliverable, a step, or a policy on the page that removes it. An obstacle with no answer is a gap.
6. **Risk sits with the seller where it honestly can.** A refund policy or guarantee the user actually honors, stated near the price, lowers the cost of saying yes.
7. **Deliverables are named by what they solve.** "Pricing calculator" says less than "the spreadsheet that tells you your day rate," when the brief supports that description.

## Guardrails For This Lens

The source book recommends scarcity, urgency, bonus stacks, and strong guarantees. In this skill:

- Scarcity and urgency only when the brief has a real deadline or limit.
- A guarantee only when the brief records a policy the user honors. Otherwise report it as an Unlock: "A stated refund policy would move risk off the buyer. Brief has none. Unlock: decide on a policy you'd honor."
- No invented bonuses, no price anchoring against tiers that don't exist, no "value stack" totals the brief can't back.

This lens is often the one that finds an offer problem rather than a copy problem. When the weakest lever is the offer, say so plainly.

## Ignores

Headline wording, tone, sentence rhythm, layout. Other lenses own those.
