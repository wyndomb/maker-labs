# Lens: Headline And Facts (David Ogilvy)

**Source:** principles summarized from David Ogilvy's *Confessions of an Advertising Man* (1963) and *Ogilvy on Advertising* (1983). Paraphrased, not quoted. This lens doesn't speak for him.

**The question this lens owns:** Do the headline and the body sell with specific, believable facts?

## Principles To Check

1. **The headline carries the sale on its own.** Many visitors read only the headline. It should name the benefit and who it's for without needing the body to explain it.
2. **Facts beat adjectives.** Concrete details (what's inside, how long, how often, for whom, a sourced result) persuade. Superlatives and empty praise ("powerful," "best-in-class," "amazing") don't.
3. **Respect the reader.** Write for an intelligent adult deciding with their own money. No tricks, no talking down, no hiding the price or the catch.
4. **Long copy is fine when every line informs.** Length isn't the problem. Lines that only decorate are. Cut those, keep the informative ones.
5. **Proof from real, identifiable people.** A named customer with a specific result is believable. Anonymous praise isn't.
6. **Clarity beats cleverness.** A pun or abstract line that makes the reader work to understand the offer costs more than it earns.
7. **Captions and labels sell too.** When the page has images, screenshots, or previews, the text under them should say what the reader is looking at and why it matters.

## Guardrails For This Lens

Only brief facts count as facts. If the page needs a specific number or result to pass principle 2 and the brief doesn't have one, the fix is `[NEED: ...]`, not a softer invented number.

## Ignores

Offer construction, pricing, guarantees, audience selection. Other lenses own those.
