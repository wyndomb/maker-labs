# Lens: Buyer Anxiety And Signals (Rory Sutherland)

**Source:** principles summarized from Rory Sutherland's *Alchemy: The Surprising Power of Ideas That Don't Make Sense* (2019). Paraphrased, not quoted. This lens doesn't speak for him.

**The question this lens owns:** What feels uncertain or risky to the buyer, even when the offer makes logical sense?

## Principles To Check

1. **People decide with psycho-logic.** Buyers act on feelings of safety, status, and doubt as much as on the features and price. A page that only argues the logic can still lose the sale.
2. **Lowering uncertainty can beat improving the offer.** Knowing what happens next, how long it takes, and how to get out often matters more to a hesitant buyer than one more feature. Look for every moment the buyer has to guess.
3. **People avoid a bad choice more than they chase the best one.** Buyers want to feel they can't go badly wrong. Show who this fits, who it doesn't, and what happens if it isn't right (only what's true).
4. **Signals that cost something build trust.** Specific detail, a real sample, a visible process, or a named person standing behind the offer is hard to fake. Generic claims cost nothing to write, so they signal little.
5. **Context changes perceived value.** The same price reads differently depending on what it sits next to. Framing should use only what the brief supports, such as the buyer's current cost of the problem when the brief records it.

## Where To Look

- The moment right before the click: does the buyer know what the button starts, what happens to their details, and what happens next?
- Price: is there an unanswered worry at the point the price appears (renewal, refund, hidden extra cost)?
- Commitment: calls, schedules, or setup that a nervous buyer would want to understand before agreeing.
- Anything that would feel embarrassing or awkward if it went wrong.

## Guardrails For This Lens

This skill deliberately leaves out cognitive-bias stacking, decoy pricing, and fake anchors. This lens looks for real doubts to answer, not nudges to apply. Every reframe has to be true and sourced in the brief.

## Ignores

Headline craft, sentence rhythm, audience selection. Other lenses own those.
