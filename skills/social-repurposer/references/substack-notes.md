# Substack Notes writer

Default full batch: 10 Notes. Use the count and angle assignments from the coordinator.

A Note is one thought, observation, realization, question, or useful instruction. It should feel like the author thinking aloud in public. It must work without reading the original source.

## Choose varied angles

Use the strongest supported options, substituting when the material lacks a particular type:

- **Thesis:** a clear position, one reason, and its implication.
- **Contrarian perspective:** a disagreement actually expressed in the source.
- **Personal realization:** a real discovery or change in judgment.
- **Mistake and lesson:** an actual failed attempt or mistaken assumption.
- **Tactical wisdom:** one small action or decision rule.
- **Prompt:** a useful source instruction or question, quoted accurately; explain why it helps. Respect any paid boundary.
- **Question flip:** a more useful question established by the source.
- **Specific example:** one concrete moment with its necessary context.
- **Stack:** three or more parallel lines, each adding a distinct step or consequence.
- **Broader implication:** what the idea changes for the reader, without unsupported predictions.

Do not force all ten types. A source without personal stories should not produce personal-story Notes.

## Shape and rhythm

Most Notes move through an opening observation, a turn, concrete substance, and a close. These are functions, not mandatory labeled sections.

Open with the actual claim, observation, realization, or source-supported tension. Avoid background, throat-clearing, vague inspiration, and generic rhetorical questions.

Move beyond the opening: what changed, what the author noticed, why something happened, or what action follows? A Note needs something beyond a quotable slogan.

Default to 4–6 short visual lines. Most lines can be under 10 words; avoid lines over 15 words when natural. Treat these as rhythm guides, not quotas that justify choppy prose or deleting a qualification. Additional lines must do distinct work.

Close with an earned reframe, quiet realization, practical consequence, genuine question, or occasional fragment. Do not bolt a question onto a finished thought. Delete the last line as a test: if nothing is lost, rewrite or remove it. Avoid formulaic contrast slogans and a second ending.

## Formatting and review

- One idea per Note; one beat per line where natural.
- No hashtags, promotional CTAs, newsletter plugs, or forced engagement.
- Minimal emphasis, no decorative formatting, no em dashes.
- Tool and product names may remain when supplied and important to the insight; generalize incidental names if clarity improves.
- Extract the useful realization from a story without retelling its whole plot or inventing emotion.
- Preserve the source's qualifications and degree of certainty.
- Check distinctness against the other Notes and against earlier drafts only when those drafts were supplied or their use was requested.

Return each complete Note with its number and type. Put source references and any concerns in a separate handoff section, not inside copy-ready text.
