## 1. Managing Blind Spots

**Purpose:** Challenge the preferred option and expose assumptions or ignored risks.

**Apply by:**

1. Identify the user's apparent preference.
2. Build the strongest case against it.
3. Surface the assumptions required for success.
4. Identify evidence that would disprove the preferred story.

**Useful questions:**

- What might the user want to be true?
- What is the strongest case for the opposite choice?
- Which assumption could cause failure?
- Who would disagree, and what evidence would they use?

Choose this lens when bias, attachment, or optimism may be shaping the choice.

## 2. Stakeholder Views

**Purpose:** Reveal effects that are hard to see from the decision maker's position.

**Apply by:**

1. Identify customers, employees, partners, investors, family, and implementers affected.
2. State what each group needs, fears, gains, or loses.
3. Surface conflicting interests.
4. Identify who must support or maintain the decision.

Choose this lens when multiple people are affected or execution depends on cooperation.

## 3. Vital Few Factors

**Purpose:** Identify the small number of factors that will determine most of the outcome.

**Apply by:**

1. List the criteria currently receiving attention.
2. Identify the two or three that truly determine success.
3. Separate requirements from preferences.
4. Remove low-impact considerations from the main decision.

Choose this lens when the decision feels overloaded, slow, or distracted by minor details.

## 4. First Principles

**Purpose:** Separate the fundamental problem from inherited solutions and conventions.

**Apply by:**

1. Define the underlying progress the business needs.
2. Separate real constraints from assumed constraints.
3. Remove the current solution from the question.
4. Rebuild the option set from what is actually required.

Choose this lens when the available options all assume the same traditional approach.

## 5. Systems Thinking

**Purpose:** Trace dependencies, feedback loops, bottlenecks, and downstream effects.

**Apply by:**

1. Map the decision's upstream inputs and downstream consequences.
2. Identify reinforcing and balancing feedback loops.
3. Find bottlenecks and maintenance burdens.
4. Identify small changes that could create a large effect.

Choose this lens when the decision affects several connected parts of the business.

## 6. Jobs to Be Done

**Purpose:** Clarify the progress the customer or business is trying to make.

**Apply by:**

1. Identify the functional job.
2. Identify emotional and social considerations.
3. Define what successful progress looks like.
4. Test whether each option actually serves that job.

Choose this lens for product, offer, positioning, customer, and buying decisions.

## 7. Opportunity Cost

**Purpose:** Make the next-best use of time, money, attention, and optionality visible.

**Apply by:**

1. Identify the resources each option consumes.
2. Name the best alternative use of those resources.
3. Separate reversible and irreversible costs.
4. Include attention, maintenance, and delayed opportunities.

Choose this lens when the choice requires meaningful commitment or closes other paths.

## 8. Regret Minimization

**Purpose:** Compare likely regret from action, inaction, and delay over time.

**Apply by:**

1. Consider one-year, five-year, and longer-term perspectives when relevant.
2. Compare regret from trying and failing with regret from never testing.
3. Identify whether the choice affects identity or a temporary tactic.
4. Check whether a small experiment can reduce both forms of regret.

Choose this lens for identity-level, time-sensitive, or difficult-to-reverse decisions.

## 9. Second-Order Effects

**Purpose:** Trace what happens after the immediate result.

**Apply by:**

1. State the first-order outcome.
2. Ask what that outcome makes more or less likely.
3. Continue to the next consequence when it is material.
4. Identify effects that compound or eventually reverse the initial benefit.

Choose this lens when the immediate answer looks attractive but may change behavior, incentives, or future options.

## 10. Inversion

**Purpose:** Identify avoidable failure modes by asking how the decision could go wrong.

**Apply by:**

1. Define failure in concrete terms.
2. List the actions or conditions that would make failure likely.
3. Identify which failure modes are preventable.
4. Build safeguards or stop conditions around them.

Choose this lens when execution risk matters as much as the choice itself.

## Relevance Labels

- **High:** The lens reveals a factor that could change the decision.
- **Medium:** The lens adds useful nuance or a condition.
- **Low:** The lens mostly confirms what is already known.

Use low relevance honestly. Skip low-value lenses in Focused Mode.
