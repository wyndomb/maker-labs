# Interview

Ask one question at a time. Five to seven in total. Skip questions their samples already answer, and stop early if answers become repetitive. Invite them to answer out loud with dictation if they can: answers then double as spoken samples.

## Question bank

Pick from these in roughly this order.

1. **Reader.** "Who do you picture reading your best piece? What do they already know, and what do you want them to do or feel at the end?"
2. **Never.** "What words, phrases, or moves make you cringe when you see them in writing, especially your own or AI-written text?"
3. **Pride.** "Which of the samples you gave me sounds most like you, and why? Which sounds least like you?"
4. **Admired writers.** "Whose writing or speaking would you happily be compared to? What do they do that you try to do too?"
5. **Opinions.** "When you disagree with a popular view in your field, how do you usually say it: head-on, with a story, with data, or with a question?"
6. **Spoken versus written.** "When friends hear you talk and then read your writing, what do they say is different?"
7. **Deliberate habits.** Ask about one or two specific patterns found in the samples: "You often open with a short personal scene. Is that deliberate, or should the file not lock it in?"

## Using the answers

- An answer is evidence like a sample, but it describes intent, not practice. When an answer conflicts with what samples show, record both and ask which one the file should follow.
- Admired writers explain direction, not rules. Do not tell another reader to imitate them. Translate the answer into a specific move this person already makes or wants to make.
- "Never" answers go straight into the Never list, quoted in their words where possible.
- Do not store interview answers verbatim beyond short phrases needed for rules. The file is about how they write, not a transcript of the interview.
